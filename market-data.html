Oil and gas royalties, music royalty payouts, and escrow market size, by the numbers

Federal and state oil and gas royalty volumes, music royalty payout totals, and escrow market size — from published sources.

Three different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures the production that generates royalties: the barrels pumped and the gas produced, which decide how much royalty money exists. The second kind measures the payout: what the royalty organizations collect, match, hold, and distribute, which decides how much money waits in escrow. The third kind measures the escrow business itself: the market for holding other people's money, and the fees charged for doing it. The first kind tells a reader how durable the royalty stream is. The second tells a reader how much of it is in transit. The third tells a reader who gets paid for holding it.

What the latest numbers show is a market where the money is large, the holding is structural, and the fees are recurring: federal royalties alone generated more than $14 billion in fiscal 2024, global recorded music revenue reached $29.6 billion the same year, and The Mechanical Licensing Collective was holding about $519.8 million in royalties pending distribution at the end of 2024 — while the escrow accounts market was estimated at $31.4 billion and projected to more than double by 2033.

How to read these numbers

Included: Energy Information Administration production and price figures for 2025; Government Accountability Office federal royalty figures for fiscal 2014–2024; Office of Natural Resources Revenue disbursement figures for fiscal 2024; Wyoming Royalty Payment Act deadlines and interest rate; IFPI global music figures for 2024; RIAA United States music figures for 2024; Spotify, ASCAP, BMI, SoundExchange, and Mechanical Licensing Collective payout and holding figures for 2024; and third-party escrow market estimates with their scopes stated. Excluded: any escrow market figure presented without its source and scope, since third-party research estimates vary widely; any royalty rate presented without its lease vintage or statutory basis; and any single payout headline presented without the holding figure beside it, since the money held is as much the story as the money paid.

Production figures are annual averages, not spot readings — the EIA's 13.6 million barrels a day is a 2025 average, and the WTI average of $65 a barrel is an annual average, not a price at any single date. Royalty disbursements are fiscal-year figures; the federal fiscal year ends September 30. Music figures mix calendar-year revenue (IFPI, RIAA) with the royalty organizations' own reporting years, so year-over-year comparisons within a row are exact and comparisons across rows are approximate. Escrow market sizes are third-party estimates: Growth Market Reports, DataHorizzon Research, and Wise Guy Reports each define the market differently, so their figures are not additive.
